## Overview
Orchha is a medieval town on the Betwa river in the Niwari district of Madhya Pradesh. It was the capital of the Bundela rulers and is known for its palaces, temples and chhatris (royal cenotaphs) along the river.

## History
Orchha was founded in the 16th century by the Bundela ruler Rudra Pratap Singh. The Jahangir Mahal was built by Bir Singh Deo in the early 17th century to honour a visit by the Mughal emperor Jahangir.

## Main monuments
The main sites are the Orchha Fort complex, which includes the Raj Mahal and the Jahangir Mahal, the Chaturbhuj Temple, and the chhatris beside the Betwa river. The Ram Raja Temple is unusual because Rama is worshipped here as a king, in a building that was once a palace.

## Visiting hours
The monuments can be visited during the daytime. Check the official Archaeological Survey of India and Madhya Pradesh Tourism websites for current timings and ticket prices before you travel.

## Best time to visit
The best time to visit is from October to March, when the weather is pleasant. During the monsoon from July to September the Betwa river is full and the area is green. Summers are hot.

## Getting there
Orchha is about 16 km from Jhansi, which is the nearest main railway station. Gwalior, about 120 km away, has the nearest airport that is commonly used. Taxis and autorickshaws run from Jhansi to Orchha.
