## Overview
Ellora is a group of 34 rock-cut monasteries and temples cut into a basalt cliff near Aurangabad (now officially Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar) in Maharashtra. It has Buddhist, Hindu and Jain monuments side by side, which shows centuries of religious harmony. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1983.

## History
The caves were carved between about the 6th and 10th centuries CE, mostly during the Rashtrakuta period. The Kailasa temple is linked to the Rashtrakuta king Krishna I in the 8th century. The 34 caves are grouped as 12 Buddhist caves, 17 Hindu caves and 5 Jain caves.

## Kailasa temple
Cave 16, the Kailasa temple, is the most famous monument at Ellora. It is a huge monolithic temple dedicated to Shiva, carved from a single rock from the top down. It was designed to look like Mount Kailasa, the home of Shiva, and is covered with detailed sculpture.

## Visiting hours and closing day
The Ellora Caves are closed on Tuesdays and open on the other days during the daytime. Check the official Archaeological Survey of India website for current timings and ticket prices before you travel.

## Best time to visit
The best time to visit is from October to March, when the weather is pleasant. During the monsoon from July to September the hills are green and waterfalls appear, but the paths can be slippery. Summers are hot, so go early in the day.

## Getting there
Ellora is about 30 km from Aurangabad, which has the nearest airport and railway station. Buses and taxis run regularly between Aurangabad and the caves.
