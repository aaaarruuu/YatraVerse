## Overview
Khajuraho is a group of Hindu and Jain temples in the Chhatarpur district of Madhya Pradesh. The temples are famous for their detailed sculptures. The Western Group of temples was named a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986.

## History
The temples were built by the Chandela dynasty between about 950 and 1050 CE. Rulers such as Yashovarman and Dhanga, and later Vidyadhara, supported the construction. There were originally around 85 temples, and about 25 survive today. After the Chandela period the site was largely forgotten under dense forest until it was brought to wider attention in the 19th century.

## Architecture and sculpture
The temples are built of sandstone in the Nagara style, with tall curved towers (shikharas) that rise like a mountain range, on high platforms. The walls carry bands of sculpture showing gods and goddesses, musicians, dancers, warriors and scenes of daily life. A small share of the carvings, often put at around ten percent, are erotic, and they are part of a much larger set of artworks.

## Main temples
The Kandariya Mahadeva Temple is the largest and most elaborate of the group and is dedicated to Shiva. The Lakshmana Temple, from about 954 CE, is among the earliest and best preserved and is dedicated to Vishnu. The Western Group is where most visitors go, and the Eastern Group includes Jain temples such as the Parshvanath Temple.

## Best time to visit
The best time to visit Khajuraho is from October to March, when the weather is pleasant for walking around the temple grounds. Summers from April to June are very hot. The Khajuraho Dance Festival, a week of classical dance performances against the temples, is usually held in February. An evening light and sound show is held at the Western Group.

## Getting there
Khajuraho has its own small airport with flights from cities such as Delhi and Varanasi. The nearest main railway stations are Khajuraho and Mahoba, and Jhansi is a large rail hub about 175 km away. Roads connect the town to Jhansi, Satna and Panna.


## Visiting hours
The Western Group of temples at Khajuraho is open to visitors on all days of the week, from sunrise to sunset. A sound and light show is held in the evening at the Western Group. Check the official Archaeological Survey of India website for current timings and ticket prices before you travel.
