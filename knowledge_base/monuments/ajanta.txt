## Overview
The Ajanta Caves are about 30 rock-cut Buddhist cave monuments in a horseshoe-shaped gorge above the Waghora river, near Aurangabad (now officially Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar) in Maharashtra. They include prayer halls (chaityas) and monasteries (viharas). The caves are a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1983.

## History
The caves were made in two phases. The first group dates from about the 2nd century BCE to the 1st century CE. The second group was carved in the 5th century CE, in the time of the Vakataka dynasty. The site was later abandoned and forgotten, until a British officer, John Smith, came across it in 1819 while hunting.

## Paintings and sculpture
Ajanta is best known for its wall paintings and sculpture, which are regarded as masterpieces of Buddhist art. The paintings show scenes from the life of the Buddha and the Jataka tales, which are stories of his earlier births. Cave 1 is well known for its paintings of Bodhisattvas.

## Visiting hours and closing day
The Ajanta Caves are closed on Mondays and open on the other days during the daytime. Flash photography is not allowed inside the caves, to protect the old paintings. Check the official Archaeological Survey of India website for current timings and ticket prices before you travel.

## Best time to visit
The best time to visit is from October to March, when the weather is cool. During the monsoon from July to September the gorge turns green, but it is humid and the paths can be slippery. Summers are hot, so go early in the day.

## Getting there
Ajanta is about 100 km from Aurangabad, which has the nearest major airport and railway station. Jalgaon, about 60 km away, is another nearby railway station. Buses and taxis run regularly from Aurangabad to the caves.
