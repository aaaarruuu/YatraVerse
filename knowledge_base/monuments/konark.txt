## Overview
The Konark Sun Temple is a 13th century temple in Konark, in the Puri district of Odisha, on the coast of the Bay of Bengal. It is dedicated to Surya, the sun god, and was named a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1984.

## History
The temple was built around 1250 CE by King Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern Ganga dynasty. European sailors called it the Black Pagoda because the main tower looked dark and was used as a landmark for ships. The main sanctum tower (vimana) collapsed over the centuries, and the porch hall (jagamohana) is the part that still stands.

## Design as a chariot
The temple is designed as a giant chariot of the sun god. It has 12 pairs of carved stone wheels, which makes 24 wheels, and the chariot is pulled by seven horses. The wheels are decorated with fine carvings, and they can be used as sundials to read the time of day.

## Architecture and sculpture
The temple is built in the Kalinga style from khondalite stone and other stones. The walls and platform carry detailed sculptures of deities, musicians, dancers, animals and scenes of daily and royal life. Some panels show erotic art, as at other temples of the period.

## Best time to visit
The best time to visit Konark is from October to March, when the weather is mild. Summers are hot and humid. The Konark Dance Festival is usually held in early December and features Odissi and other classical dances with the temple as the backdrop. Chandrabhaga Beach is close to the temple.

## Getting there
Konark is about 35 km from Puri and about 65 km from Bhubaneswar, which has the nearest airport and a major railway station. Buses and taxis run regularly between these cities and Konark.
