## Overview
Sanchi is a Buddhist site in the Raisen district of Madhya Pradesh, about 46 km from Bhopal. It is known for the Great Stupa, one of the oldest stone structures in India, and it was named a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1989.

## History
The Great Stupa (Stupa 1) was first built by the Mauryan emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE, and it held relics of the Buddha. It was enlarged and covered in stone later, and the four decorated gateways (toranas) were added in the 1st century BCE during the Satavahana period. The site was in poor condition for centuries and was restored in the early 20th century under John Marshall.

## The gateways and carvings
The four toranas face the four directions and are covered in carvings of scenes from the Buddha's life and from the Jataka tales. In early Buddhist art the Buddha is not shown in human form. He is shown through symbols such as the Bodhi tree, the wheel, footprints and an empty throne.

## Other monuments at the site
Besides the Great Stupa, the hill has Stupa 2 and Stupa 3, ruined monasteries, temples and an Ashoka pillar. Stupa 3 is near the Great Stupa and once held relics of two chief disciples of the Buddha, Sariputta and Maha Moggallana.

## Best time to visit
The best time to visit Sanchi is from October to March, when the weather is cool. The monsoon from July to September makes the hill green, but the paths can be slippery. Summers are hot, so go early in the day. The site is open during the day.

## Getting there
Sanchi has its own small railway station, and Vidisha is the nearest larger town about 10 km away. Bhopal has the nearest airport and is a major rail hub, and buses and taxis run from Bhopal to Sanchi.
